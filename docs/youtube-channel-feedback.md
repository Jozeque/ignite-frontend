## The framework I'm applying

Every recommendation in this doc traces back to one of these sources. I'm naming them inline so you can sanity-check the reasoning.

### Source 1 — User-shared reference: "9 YouTube Settings That Kill Small Channels (Fix These Now)" (`CghjIJ3wdZA`)
This video is part of an entire genre (also covered exhaustively by Jordan Kruk's "50 settings" piece and Mediacube's "20 hidden settings" article). The shared takeaway across the genre:
- **Settings hygiene compounds.** Channel keywords, video category, end screens, custom chapters, default visibility, location, and watermarks each cost ~1 minute to fix and each gives the algorithm one more correct signal. None of them individually move the needle. All of them together do.
- **Manual control beats defaults.** "Publish to subscription feed and notify subscribers" should be off until you're sure the video is a hit — auto-blasting subs with weak content trains them to ignore your notifications.
- **Default to Unlisted, not Public.** Lets you inspect the upload, set a thumbnail, write the description, then go live deliberately.
- **Subscribe-confirmation hack.** Append `?sub_confirmation=1` to your channel link in every description — surfaces the YouTube subscribe popup automatically.
- **Channel keywords field is free SEO.** Most small channels leave it blank. Add 30+ relevant terms (Ableton, Max for Live, sound design, modulation, automation, techno, house, bass, electronic music production, racks, instrument racks, sound design tutorial, etc.).

### Source 2 — Paddy Galloway (Outlier Strategy / Creator Science podcast)
Galloway is the most-cited working YouTube strategist (clients include MrBeast, Trahan, etc.).
- **CCN Fit:** Every video must work for Core fans, Casual viewers, and brand New audiences. Tutorials that only speak to existing Stride users are dead on arrival.
- **Packaging is 40% of the game.** "Most spend days/weeks on the video and less than 5% of time on the thumbnail and title." That ratio has to flip.
- **Test 3 thumbnails per video.** Sketch 15, narrow to 3 distinct concepts, A/B test via YouTube's native Test & Compare.
- **Title under 50 characters,** simple words, emotionally resonant. Long descriptive titles signal "tutorial only people already searching for this will click."
- **One-page idea framework:** rough thumb sketch + proposed title + 1-3 reasons why it works + logline. If you can't write a one-pager, the video isn't ready to shoot.

### Source 3 — MrBeast (leaked strategy doc + creator analysis pieces)
The three metrics MrBeast obsesses over: **CTR, Average View Duration (AVD), Average View Percentage (AVP).**
- **First 60 seconds are everything.** Front-load payoff, deliver on the title's promise immediately, use quick cuts.
- **Thumbnail formula: One Face, One Object, One Question.** Strip everything else.
- **Extreme thesis = higher CTR** (provided the video delivers). "I almost died in the desert" beats "I spent 24 hours in the desert."
- **Update old thumbnails as your design improves.** Refreshed thumbs unlock dormant reach.
- **Quality over quantity.** One 5M-view video > 100 videos at 50K each.

### Source 4 — Jay Alto (works with MrBeast, Ryan Trahan, Colin & Samir)
- **4 elements of a great thumbnail:** clear focal subject, emotional cue, contextual stake, readability at smallest size.
- **Storytelling > tactics.** Every video is a setup → conflict → payoff. Tutorials usually fail because they're all payoff and no setup.
- **Channel-level pattern.** When viewers can recognize your thumbnail style in 0.1s, every future upload benefits from the previous one.

### Source 5 — Tutorial channel growth research (Onewrk / EntreResource 2025-2026 playbooks)
Tutorial-channel-specific principles, not general creator advice:
- **Angle beats topic.** "Build a 6-figure Notion template business — case study" beats "How to make money with Notion." Same content, completely different click intent.
- **Educational : Entertainment : Inspirational ≈ 40 / 35 / 25.** Tutorial channels that hit only the educational bucket plateau fast.
- **Shorts are the discovery layer.** Most new subs to small channels in 2026 come from Shorts, not long videos. Long videos are for retention/conversion. You currently have zero Shorts.
- **TikTok/Instagram Reels as feeders.** End every short-form post with "full walkthrough on YouTube."

## Thumbnail guidelines

### Color palette (lock these — never improvise)
- **Primary background:** #09090b (zinc-950, the brand dark)
- **Accent gradient:** orange #fb923c → fuchsia #d946ef (the canvas curve color in-app)
- **Text:** white #fafafa or the orange when contrast allows
- **Forbidden:** pure red (YT favicon collision), pure blue (Ableton's brand), gradients with green
- The reason: thumbnail recognition compounds. After 10 uploads with the same palette, viewers spot you at any feed position.

### Text rules
- **Max 4 words on screen.** "ONE PATCH 50 STABS" beats "How I made 50 stabs from one Serum patch with Stride."
- **Font:** Outfit Black (matches the app font — brand consistency from product into thumb).
- **Size:** text fills 35-45% of the thumb width. If you can't read it on a phone in feed, redo.
- **Contrast:** dark background, light text, or vice versa. No mid-grey on mid-grey.

### What to show
- **Always:** one striking visual element — usually a Stride canvas curve or an Ableton interface fragment.
- **Sometimes:** a face crop (corner, not center). Adds 10-30% CTR over time once viewers recognize you. Even just eyes.
- **Sometimes:** the product transformation as a split — left "before" / right "after" with an arrow.
- **For genre-specific videos:** include a recognizable genre cue (vinyl, kick drum waveform, modular patch cable, etc.)

### What to AVOID
- Generic Ableton screenshots with no overlay
- Stock images of headphones / studios / waveforms
- Logos in the center (logos go bottom-right small or nowhere)
- More than 2 colors (orange + dark + white IS the palette)
- Anything that looks like a Splice ad
- Faces with neutral expressions (excited, surprised, focused-determined — pick one)
- Red, white, and black combos (per Hootsuite/MrBeast: those colors clash with YT's UI)

### Reference niche channels to study (same audience, study their thumbnails)
- **Andrew Huang** — face-driven, single-prop, color-coded series
- **Mr Bill** — text-heavy, technical, dense (works for him because audience = sound design heads)
- **Tom Cosm** — clean, single-screenshot, brand-consistent
- **Ableton (official)** — minimal, screenshot-led — what to avoid (too understated for a small channel)
- **Sidebrain** — high-contrast, big text, often single Ableton screenshot with overlay — closest to the lane Stride should occupy

## Content strategy

### 4 content pillars (the rotation)

1. **Sound Design Walkthroughs (40%)** — "I made X from Y." The discovery engine. Genre-tagged.
2. **Template Deep Dives (25%)** — one per template, evergreen, the SEO backbone. "Pump explained," "Glitch in 5 minutes," etc.
3. **Workflow Tips / Quick Wins (20%)** — 60-second Shorts + occasional 2-3 min long-form. The cadence keeper.
4. **Behind the Build (15%)** — origin stories, roadmap reveals, "why I'm building this for one-time purchase not subscription." Builds parasocial trust → trial conversion.

### Series ideas (start one, ship 5 episodes before evaluating)

- **"60 Seconds with Stride"** — daily-ish Shorts. One curve, one transformation, one demo. Lowest production cost, highest cadence value.
- **"From Scratch"** — empty rack → finished track-ready patch in one video. Long-form, ~10-15min. Producer-language pure.
- **"Real Producers Test Stride"** — invite a friend producer, record their first session. Social proof video. Massive trust value.
- **"Patch Rescue"** — viewers send their boring presets, you transform them with Stride. Community engagement + content fuel.
- **"Template Deep Dive"** — the SEO series. One per template. These are evergreen — they rank forever.

### Cadence target

**Realistic for solo founder:**
- 1 long-form per week (sound design walkthrough or template deep dive)
- 2-3 Shorts per week (60-second tips, Reels reuse)
- 1 community post per week (poll, BTS, work-in-progress)

**Result expected:** in 90 days, you have ~12 long-form + ~30 Shorts + the playlist scaffold filled in. That's enough surface area for the algorithm to start recommending you in-niche.

### Viewer → trial → buyer funnel

- **Top of funnel:** Shorts and search-discoverable template videos (catch the "ableton sound design serum" searcher).
- **Mid-funnel:** Walkthrough videos with chapters and obvious "free trial" CTA in description + pinned comment.
- **Bottom-funnel:** Channel trailer + About description doing the closing work; long-form behind-the-build videos that build trust.
- **Conversion event:** "Try free" link in every description, every pinned comment, every end screen. Lemon Squeezy checkout.
