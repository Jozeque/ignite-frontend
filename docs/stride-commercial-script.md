# STRIDE — Commercial Strategy + VO Scripts

Built for the fixed reel (`docs/stride-commercial-fixed.html`): real Ableton track column on top, StrideLink docked at the bottom, the clip becomes the lanes on each fire. ~35s, loopable, can stretch to ~45s for breathing room.

Copy rules: never mention AI. Lead with sound design, MIDI second. Producer language. No em dashes. No hype words. Talk producer-to-producer.

**LOSER — "Hook 2" (didn't convert):**

**Why the winner won (the five rules):**

1. **Command, not a question.** "Stop drawing automation by hand" beats "Wasting time drawing automation?" A command assumes authority. A question invites a swipe.
2. **One specific number beats five vague features.** "100 automation lines to every parameter, one click" is visceral. "Dozens of unique automations" is fog. The brain can picture 100. It can't picture "dozens."
3. **A credibility line.** "I've been exploring sounds for 10 years, and for me it's a game changer." That single sentence is the whole ad's trust. The loser has zero credibility, just a feature list.
4. **It breathes.** The winner front-loads all VO into the first 19 seconds, says "check this out," then shuts up for 24 seconds and lets the demo + music carry. The loser talks nonstop for 30 seconds and exhausts you. Silence sells the product.
5. **A real CTA with a reason to act now.** "Use this code for 20% off" closes. "You can modulate everything in your session" just trails off.

Every script below is built on these five rules.

## 1 · CMO directions — VETERANS

Audience: producers who already know how to route an LFO to a macro, one at a time, and who know the dead feeling of a rack they love but can't get a fresh variation out of. Sell them **speed, scale, and surprise** — never "easy." Respect their craft. Lead with a pain they live daily.

### V1 · "Stop drawing it by hand" (the proven winner, sharpened)
- **Insight:** This is the exact angle that already converted. Don't reinvent it, sharpen it. Name the tedium they do every session and yank it away.
- **Hook line:** "Stop drawing automation by hand. You're spending hours your track will never thank you for." / alt: "You've drawn the same automation curve a thousand times. Watch this instead."
- **Visual (first 3s):** your real `tedious_automation_in_ableton.mp4` (the one-lane-at-a-time mouse drag) on screen, ticking clock SFX, then a hard cut to one Stride press lighting up every lane at once.

### V2 · "An hour of work in two seconds" (the receipts)
- **Insight:** Veterans respect horsepower they can count. Make the math the flex. The on-screen counter (10 params, ~38,000 points, one click) IS the ad.
- **Hook line:** "This would take you an hour. Watch it happen in two seconds." / alt: "Ten parameters. Thirty-eight thousand points of movement. One click."
- **Visual:** split-second of a clock / empty automation lanes, then the press, then the counter spinning up fast while every lane fills. Let the number land on screen.

### V3 · "Same rack, infinite variations" (the rut)
- **Insight:** Their deeper pain is not setup time, it is being stuck. They built the patch, mapped the LFOs, and now they can't get a genuinely new version without tearing it down. This is their literal complaint: locked into the setup they made.
- **Hook line:** "Your favorite rack has a hundred sounds you've never heard. Here's how to pull them out." / alt: "Same rack. One button. A different variation every time."
- **Visual:** one clip, then fire, fire, fire, the curves rewriting into a new shape each press (Mutate, Prism, reroll). The point is "it never repeats." Kills the urge to go buy another plugin.

> **CMO pick for veterans:** run **V1** as the cold workhorse (it already works), test **V3** against it for the warm / retargeting audience (rut-breaking is a stronger second-touch message than tedium).

## 2 · CMO directions — BEGINNERS

Audience: newer producers who want their tracks to sound professional and alive, who follow artists they want to sound like, and who are shopping for the tool that finally levels them up. Sell them **aspiration, a visible result, and zero complexity.** Don't teach mechanics. Show the transformation and the identity.

### B1 · "This is why your tracks sound flat" (diagnosis to cure)
- **Insight:** Beginners can hear that their track sounds amateur but can't name why. Name the invisible problem for them, then be the one-click cure. You become the person who finally explained it.
- **Hook line:** "Your sounds aren't bad. They just don't move. That's the whole difference." / alt: "The gap between your track and a pro's? Movement. Here's the shortcut."
- **Visual:** a flat, static loop with a dead-straight automation line, then the press, then everything starts breathing.

### B2 · "Before / after" (the transformation)
- **Insight:** Beginners buy with their ears and eyes, not specs. The dramatic A/B is the most shareable, most thumb-stopping format there is. No explanation required.
- **Hook line:** "Watch a boring loop turn into a record." / alt: spoken minimal: "Before. [press] After." and let the audio do the talking.
- **Visual:** boring clip plays 2s, big press, the clip blooms into moving lanes and the sound transforms. This is the one most likely to travel organically.

### B3 · "The unfair advantage" (level-up / identity)
- **Insight:** Their words: "the next tool that's going to improve me." Lean straight into that. Position Stride as the cheat code that makes them sound like the producers they look up to, without years of theory.
- **Hook line:** "If you're just starting out, this is the unfair advantage nobody handed you." / alt: "The shortcut to sounds you didn't think you were good enough to make yet."
- **Visual:** fast, hype-paced montage of presses and transforming clips, the counter climbing, ending on the result. Energy and aspiration over explanation.

> **CMO pick for beginners:** lead with **B2** (before/after travels furthest and is cheapest to serve cold), use **B3** as the retargeting / identity closer once they've seen the transformation.

## 4 · Production notes

- **Pacing is the feature.** Keep all VO inside the first ~19s, then go quiet. If a line feels rushed, stretch the reel beat under it (raise `FDUR` in the reel, or hold the first fire longer) rather than talking faster. Silence over the demo is doing work.
- **The counter is a hook, not decoration.** Make sure it is readable when the VO says "every parameter" / "automation curve."
- **CTA needs a reason now.** A discount code is what closed the winner. Keep it. Burn it on screen at the end too, codes get screenshotted.
- **One variable per test.** Keep this body + demo + CTA constant. Only swap the first 3 seconds (the hook) between the six directions above. That's how you learn which hook wins without muddying the read.
- **Burned captions, always.** Most of the feed is muted. The first line has to land as text on frame 1.

## 5 · Real UI vs the simulated reel (the trust question)

Your instinct is correct, and your own data backs it. The winner showed the real Stride UI, and for skeptical producers, real screen footage reads as "this is an actual tool," while polished motion graphics can read as "concept." Authenticity converts here. So the simulated reel should not be the only thing they see.

The good news: StrideLink (the device) now holds everything, so you do not need the canvas to make it real. A clean screen recording of the device in Ableton is your trust shot, and it did not even exist when you shot the winner.

**The play: use both, each for what it is good at.**
- **Simulated reel = the explainer + the money shot.** It makes the mechanic legible in a way a raw screen grab cannot: the clip visibly becoming the automation lanes. Use it to dazzle and to teach, especially the clip-to-lanes morph.
- **Real StrideLink screen recording = the trust.** Cut to the real device in a real session: real press, real clip filling with real automation. This is what makes a pro believe it.
- **17s real-time outputs = the proof.** The sound. This is the conversion engine, same as the winner's silent stretch.

**Recommended demo backbone:** real StrideLink screen rec as the spine, with the simulated reel layered in as the stylized accent for the clip-to-lanes transformation, then the 17s of real outputs. Bridge them with continuity: end a simulated beat on a clip, hard cut to that same clip playing for real in Ableton with sound. "What you just saw is real."

**Is the simulated footage good enough for the new script?** As the explainer and the eye-candy, yes, it is strong and on-brand. As the sole proof, no, and not because it is weak but because your winner proved real UI builds trust this audience needs. Pair it with real StrideLink footage and real outputs, and you get the dazzle and the belief in one cut.

One cleanup: since the live cut already has a filmed hook and a CTA card, the simulated reel does not need to carry its own IT'S ENDLESS / logo outro. Let the filmed closer own the CTA so you are not closing twice.
