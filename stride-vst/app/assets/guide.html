STRIDE

Getting Started — 7 Steps

1
Load your instrument

Build a synth from scratch or load an existing rack/preset onto a MIDI track in Ableton.

2
Map your parameters

Every parameter you want Stride to control needs a single automation point on its lane — just enough to tell Ableton "embed this envelope in the clip." The fastest way: arm recording and touch each knob once.

Fastest way (recommended)

1. Hit F9 to arm automation recording.

2. Nudge every knob you want Stride to control — Ableton drops an automation point on each one you touch.

3. Hit F9 again to disarm. Done.

Or, manually

Show the automation lane for each parameter → click once on each lane to drop a single point. Slower, but works if you don't want to arm recording.

Important

Every parameter needs at least one automation point on its lane. Without a point, Ableton won't embed the envelope and Stride can't write to that parameter.

3
Drop StrideLink on the same track

Drag StrideLink.amxd from the M4L folder onto the same MIDI track, after your instrument.

4
Save the clip as a template

Drag the MIDI clip (the clip slot with your recorded automation) to the User Library in Ableton's browser sidebar. Stride detects the new .alc file automatically and imports it as your template.

Drag the clip, not the rack

You must drag the clip — the colored block in the clip slot — not the rack/device title bar. Only the clip contains the automation envelopes that Stride needs. If you drag the rack, the template will be missing envelopes.

What happens

Stride watches your User Library folder. When the .alc file lands there, the canvas shows a green confirmation — your template is saved to ~/Desktop/Stride/template/ and ready to use.

5
Click Scan Mapped

In StrideLink (or the canvas sidebar), click Scan Mapped. This reads all parameters that have automation lanes and loads them into the canvas.

6
Open Canvas — get wild

The Stride canvas launches with your parameters as lanes — one lane per mapped knob. Draw curves freehand, apply shape templates (Sine, Pump, Glitch, Groove, Chaos, Neuro), use Chaos to fill every lane at once, Bloom to spread one curve across all lanes with variations, or Weave for phase-shifted counterpoint.

Shape each lane with the sidebar sliders — Smooth, Depth, Curve, Floor, Ceiling. Use Select All or Select mode to apply tools to multiple lanes at once. Lock any lane you want to protect from changes.

Pick your loop length up top (2/4/8/16 bars), name your clip in the input field, then click Apply to Clip.

7
Drag the output into Ableton

After you hit Apply, your generation appears in the Recent Generations dock at the bottom of Stride. Grab any card from the dock and drag it straight into an empty clip slot in Ableton — the slot above the device area on the right side, NOT into the rack itself.

If your curves don't apply

Drop the .alc onto a fresh empty MIDI track with no rack on it (the slot will say "Drop an Instrument or Sample Here" below). Don't drop it onto the same track that has your rack — that can silently fail because Ableton tries to match the existing devices and gets confused. New empty track every time. It just works.

Keep going

Tweak your curves, hit Apply again — each apply adds a new card to the dock. Drop 3-5 variations in a row, then A/B them in Ableton.

Need the file?

Every .alc is also saved to ~/Desktop/Stride/. Hit Open folder on the bottom-right toast to browse them in Explorer.
