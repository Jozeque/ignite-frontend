STRIDE

Getting Started — 7 Steps

1
Load your instrument

Build a synth from scratch or load an existing rack/preset onto a MIDI track in Ableton.

2
Map your parameters

Open automation lanes for every parameter you want Stride to control and draw a single point on each lane — just one click per lane is enough.

How to do it

1. Show the automation lane for each parameter you want to control.

2. Click once on each lane to draw a single automation point — this tells Ableton to embed that envelope in the template.

3. Create a MIDI clip on the track (double-click a clip slot).

Important

Every parameter needs at least one automation point drawn on its lane. Do not use MIDI Map + Record — draw the points manually. Without a point on the lane, Ableton won't embed the envelope and Stride can't write to that parameter.

3
Drop StrideLink on the same track

Drag StrideLink.amxd from the M4L folder onto the same MIDI track, after your instrument.

4
Save the clip as a template

Drag the MIDI clip (the clip slot with your recorded automation) to the User Library in Ableton's browser sidebar. Stride detects the new .alc file automatically and imports it as your template.

Drag the clip, not the rack

You must drag the clip — the colored block in the clip slot — not the rack/device title bar. Only the clip contains the automation envelopes that Stride needs. If you drag the rack, the template will be missing envelopes.

What happens

Stride watches your User Library folder. When the .alc file lands there, the canvas shows a green confirmation — your template is saved to ~/Desktop/Stride/template/ and ready to use.

5
Click Scan Mapped

In StrideLink (or the canvas sidebar), click Scan Mapped. This reads all parameters that have automation lanes and loads them into the canvas.

6
Open Canvas — get wild

The Stride canvas launches with your parameters as lanes. Draw curves, apply templates, use Chaos to fill all lanes at once, or Bloom to spread one curve across every lane with variations.

Name your clip in the input field, then click Apply to Clip.

7
Drag the output .alc into Ableton

Stride generates a new .alc file in ~/Desktop/Stride/ and opens the folder for you. Drag it onto a new MIDI track — your rack loads with all the automation baked in.

Keep going

Go back to the canvas, tweak your curves, hit Apply again. Each apply creates a new .alc — iterate fast until you find the sound you want.
